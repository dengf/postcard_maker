Privacy Policy

Last updated 28 August 2026

Postcard Maker has no server, no accounts, and no analytics. Your photo is never uploaded, and nothing you write on it is transmitted anywhere.

What we collect

Nothing. There is no account to create, no form that submits anywhere, and no tracking or analytics of any kind — no cookies, no pixels, no third-party scripts.

How the photo is processed

Cropping, filters, and image encoding are compiled from Rust to WebAssembly and run inside your browser. Your photo — whether taken with your camera or chosen from your device — never leaves it. The greeting message and any stickers you place are drawn onto the image locally as well, using your browser's own canvas.

Your unfinished postcard

The photo and settings for a postcard you haven't finished yet are saved to your browser's IndexedDB storage so a reload doesn't lose your work. It is local to that browser on that device, not synced or backed up anywhere. Clearing your browser's site data deletes it permanently, and we have no copy.

Sharing or saving the finished postcard

"Share" hands the finished image to your device's own share sheet or mail client — we never see who you send it to, or the message you write. "Save image" downloads the file straight to your device. Both actions happen entirely between your browser and your device.

Hosting

The site is served as static files by GitHub Pages. Like any web server, GitHub's infrastructure handles the network requests needed to deliver the page and may log technical details such as IP addresses. That is standard web hosting and is outside our control; it is covered by GitHub's privacy statement. We receive no data from it and have no access to those logs.

Beyond loading the page itself, the app makes no network requests that send your photo, message, or anything else out — with one exception, described next. You can verify this in your browser's developer tools: open the Network tab and make a postcard.

Third-party models

"Suggest a look" downloads two models the first time you tap that button, and only then — about 11MB together: a photo-classification model — MobileNetV3-Small, trained on ImageNet-1000, licensed BSD-3-Clause — and a face-counting model — Ultra-Light-Fast-Generic-Face-Detector-1MB, licensed MIT, used only to count face-shaped regions in a photo — never to identify, recognize, or analyze anyone. Both downloads are one-way fetches of the model files themselves; your photo never goes anywhere. Both models then run entirely in your browser, the same as every other calculation in this app.

Both ship exactly as they were downloaded from their projects — no change to either model's architecture or its trained weights. Full attribution, including the required copyright notices, is in MODEL-LICENSES.md.

Open source components

Beyond those two models, the app is built from open-source software — the Rust crates compiled into the WebAssembly engine, and the JavaScript libraries that render the page. Their licenses require that their copyright notices travel with the code, so the full license text for every one of them is on the open source licenses page. None of them is a network service: they are compiled into the files your browser already downloaded.

Children

The app is a general-purpose creative tool and is not directed at children. Since no data is collected from anyone, none is collected from children either.

Changes

If this policy ever changes, the revised version will be published here with a new date above. Because the app collects nothing, any change that introduced data collection would be a significant one and called out in the app itself.
